How to read the flow-rate columns

A tankless heater does not have a single GPM rating. Its output depends on how far it has to raise the incoming water temperature. The colder your supply water, the fewer gallons per minute the same unit delivers.

  • Temperature rise = your target output temperature minus the incoming ground water temperature.
  • Add up the flow of the fixtures you expect to run at the same time, then read the column that matches your temperature rise.
  • Northern installations generally size against the higher rise columns; warmer regions can size against the lower ones.

In a combi model number, the second figure is the heating input in thousands of BTU/h: NCB-240/110H has 110,000 BTU/h of space heating and 199,900 BTU/h of domestic hot water input. Notes on selection

  • NPE-A2 vs NPE-S2 — both share the same gas input and flow rates. The A2 adds Navien’s built-in ComfortFlow recirculation system with an internal buffer tank and pump; the S2 does not, and is the lower-cost option when an external recirculation loop is already planned.
  • Condensing vs non-condensing — the NPE series runs at UEF 0.93 to 0.96 and vents in PVC. The NPN and NHW series run at UEF 0.81 and carry different venting requirements. Check the exhaust material listed on each product page before quoting a vent run.
  • Gas supply — every model in this table lists its required natural gas and propane supply pressure on its product page. Confirm the meter and line can carry the input before specifying.
