A Honeywell thermostat with a blank screen has lost power. On battery models the cause is almost always dead batteries. On hardwired models the 24 VAC supply from the furnace or air handler has been interrupted, most often by a tripped breaker, a furnace door that is not fully closed, a blown fuse on the control board or a tripped condensate float switch. Work through the checks below in order, from the simplest to the ones that need a multimeter. They apply to Honeywell Home thermostats made by Resideo, including the PRO, FocusPRO and T-series.

Honeywell thermostat blank screen: quick checks

  1. Replace the batteries with fresh alkaline cells.
  2. Check that the thermostat is fully seated on its wallplate.
  3. Check the furnace or air handler breaker and the service switch.
  4. Close the furnace blower door firmly.
  5. Check the low-voltage fuse on the furnace control board.
  6. Look for water in the condensate pan or a tripped float switch.
  7. Check the R and C wires, then measure for 24 VAC.
  8. Restart the thermostat. If it has power and stays blank, replace it.

If you are not sure, pull the thermostat straight off its wallplate. Look for a battery compartment and check whether a wire is connected to the terminal marked C.

Step 1: Replace the batteries

Replace all the batteries with new alkaline cells of the size marked in the compartment, check the polarity, and clean any corrosion from the contacts. Snap the thermostat back onto the wallplate until it clicks. Most models show a low battery warning for weeks before the display goes blank, so a screen that went dark without warning points to the HVAC power supply.

Step 2: Check the breaker and the furnace switch

A hardwired thermostat is powered by a transformer inside the furnace or air handler. If that equipment has no power, the screen is blank.

  • Reset the furnace or air handler breaker once if it has tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
  • Find the service switch on or near the furnace. It looks like a light switch and is easily turned off by mistake.
  • In a heat pump or air conditioning system, the indoor air handler has its own breaker, separate from the outdoor unit. The thermostat is powered from the indoor unit.

Step 3: Close the furnace door

Furnaces have a door safety switch that cuts power when the blower door is removed. If the door was not seated properly after a filter change, the switch stays open, the transformer has no power and the thermostat goes blank. Refit the door so it presses the switch fully. Do not tape or wedge the switch closed.

Step 4: Check the fuse on the control board

Most furnace and air handler control boards carry an automotive-style blade fuse, commonly 3 A or 5 A, that protects the 24 V circuit. It blows when two thermostat wires touch or a wire shorts to the cabinet, which often happens during a thermostat change.

  1. Turn off power to the furnace at the service switch or breaker.
  2. Remove the door and find the fuse on the control board.
  3. Pull the fuse and look at the metal link. A broken link means it has blown.
  4. Replace it only with a fuse of the same amp rating printed on the board. Never fit a larger fuse or bridge the holder.
  5. Before restoring power, look for the cause: bare wire touching metal, a pinched cable, stray strands at the thermostat, or damaged wire at the outdoor unit.

If the new fuse blows again, there is a short in the low-voltage wiring or a failed component. That needs a technician.

Step 5: Check the condensate float switch

Air conditioners, heat pumps and condensing furnaces produce condensate. Many systems have a float switch in the drain pan, drain line or condensate pump that shuts the system down when water backs up. On many installations the switch is wired in series with the R wire, so a full pan removes power from the thermostat. If you find standing water, clear the drain line, empty the pan and let the switch reset. Do not bypass the switch: it is there to prevent water damage. A drain that keeps clogging needs service.

Step 6: Check the wiring and measure the voltage

  • At the thermostat, each wire should be tight in its terminal with no bare copper touching a neighbor.
  • At the control board, the R and C connections should be tight.
  • With a multimeter set to AC volts, measure between R and C at the wallplate. A healthy system reads about 24 to 28 VAC.

No voltage with the breaker on, the door closed and a good fuse means a broken wire or a failed transformer. If a Wi-Fi thermostat has no C wire at all, it needs one. Our thermostat C-wire guide covers the options, including an adapter such as the Honeywell Home THP9045A1023/U WireSaver, which is installed at the equipment.

Step 7: Restart the thermostat

If power is confirmed and the screen is still blank or frozen, pull the thermostat off the wallplate, remove the batteries, wait one minute, then refit the batteries and the thermostat. Model-specific resets, including factory resets on T-series and Wi-Fi models, are in how to reset a Honeywell thermostat. A factory reset erases installer settings such as system type, so record them first.

When should you replace the thermostat?

Replace it when it has fresh batteries or a confirmed 24 VAC between R and C and the display still does not come on after a restart, or when the display is cracked or there is corrosion or water damage inside. Match the replacement to the system: number of heating and cooling stages, conventional or heat pump, and whether a C wire is present.

When to call a professional

  • The breaker or the control board fuse trips or blows a second time.
  • There is no 24 VAC between R and C and the cause is not the door, fuse or float switch. Transformer and control board replacement involves 120 V or 240 V wiring.
  • The thermostat is a line voltage model (120 V or 240 V, used with electric baseboard heat). Those are not covered here and need an electrician.
  • You see burned wiring, a scorched control board or water inside the furnace.
  • You smell gas. Leave the building and call the gas utility from outside.

Always switch off power to the furnace or air handler before opening it. Thermostat wiring is 24 V, but the equipment it connects to is not.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Honeywell thermostat blank after changing the batteries?

Check battery polarity and that the thermostat is fully snapped onto the wallplate. If the model is hardwired, the fault is in the 24 VAC supply: breaker, service switch, door switch, fuse or float switch.

Why did the screen go blank after I installed a new thermostat?

The usual cause is a blown 3 A or 5 A fuse on the furnace control board from wires touching during installation. The other is a missing C wire on a model that requires one.

Can a clogged AC drain make the thermostat go blank?

Yes, where the float switch interrupts the R wire. Clear the drain and the pan and the switch resets.

Does a blank screen mean the thermostat is broken?

Usually not. Replace the thermostat only after confirming that it has power.
