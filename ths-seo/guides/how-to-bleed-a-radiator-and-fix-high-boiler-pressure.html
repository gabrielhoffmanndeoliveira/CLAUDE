To bleed a radiator, turn the heating off and let the system cool, open the bleed valve at the top of the radiator a quarter to half turn with a radiator key, let the air hiss out, and close the valve as soon as a steady stream of water appears. Then check the boiler pressure gauge and top the system up if it has dropped. If the boiler pressure is too high instead, the usual causes are a failed expansion tank, a fill valve that leaks through, or overfilling. This guide covers hot water (hydronic) systems only. Steam radiators have automatic air vents and are not bled this way.

How do you know a radiator needs bleeding?

  • The radiator is warm at the bottom and cool at the top.
  • You hear gurgling or the sound of running water in radiators or baseboard.
  • Upper-floor radiators heat poorly while lower ones are hot.

A radiator that is cold all over usually has a different problem: a closed radiator valve, a stuck zone valve or a circulator that is not running.

What you need

  • A radiator key, such as the Pasco radiator valve key, or a flat screwdriver for slotted vents
  • A rag and a small container
  • A boiler pressure gauge you can read. Replacements are in temperature and pressure gauges.

How to bleed a radiator, step by step

  1. Find the cold spots. Run the heating until the radiators are warm and note which ones are cool at the top.
  2. Turn the heating off. Set the thermostat down so the circulator stops, and let the radiators cool until they are comfortable to touch. Water in a hot system can scald.
  3. Read the boiler pressure. Note the gauge reading. The system needs positive pressure to push the air out.
  4. Start on the lowest floor and work upward.
  5. Find the bleed valve. It is a small fitting near the top of the radiator, at one end.
  6. Open it slowly. Hold the rag and container under the vent and turn the key counterclockwise a quarter to half turn. Air will hiss out. Do not unscrew the stem completely.
  7. Close it when water flows. When water comes out in a steady stream with no sputtering, close the vent snugly. Do not overtighten.
  8. Repeat on every radiator with a vent.
  9. Check the pressure again and top up if needed (see below). Turn the heating on and confirm the radiators heat evenly.

A bleed valve that is corroded or drips after closing should be replaced. A coin-operated vent such as the Taco 417-3 coin vent (1/8 in. NPT) is a common replacement. Check the thread size first. The radiator must be isolated or the system drained below that level before a vent is unscrewed.

How do you bleed baseboard heat?

Most fin-tube baseboard has no bleed valve on each section. Some systems have a manual vent on an elbow under the end cover; if yours does, bleed it like a radiator. Otherwise air is removed by purging each zone with fresh water through a purge valve near the boiler: the zone's shut-off is closed, a hose is connected to the purge valve, and the fill valve's fast-fill lever pushes water through the loop until the hose runs free of air. The boiler must be off and cool, and the pressure gauge has to be watched throughout so it stays well below the relief valve setting. If you are not familiar with the valves on your boiler, have a heating technician purge it.

Can you stop bleeding by hand?

Automatic air vents release air on their own at high points and on air separators. A float inside opens the vent when air collects and closes it when water returns. An example is the Taco 400-4 Hy-Vent (1/8 in. NPT). For a system that collects air repeatedly, an air separator on the boiler supply removes dissolved air as the water heats. Browse all air vents.

What should boiler pressure be?

ConditionTypical readingMeaning
Cold systemAbout 12 to 15 psiNormal fill pressure for a one or two story house
Hot systemRises several psi, commonly to around 20 psiNormal expansion
Approaching 30 psiToo highMost residential boiler relief valves open at 30 psi
Below about 10 psi coldToo lowUpper radiators may not fill, and air can be drawn in

These are general figures. Fill pressure depends on the height of the system: each foot of height above the gauge needs 0.43 psi, plus a few psi at the top. Use the range in your boiler manual.

How do you top up boiler pressure after bleeding?

  1. Check that the valve on the cold water feed to the boiler is open. An automatic fill valve, such as the Taco 329-3 or the Resideo FM911/U combination fill valve and backflow preventer, brings the system back to its set pressure on its own.
  2. If the pressure is still low, lift the fast-fill lever briefly while watching the gauge, and release it at the normal cold pressure.
  3. On a combi boiler, use the built-in fill device as described in its manual. See the combi boiler guide.

A system that needs topping up often has a leak. Find it, because fresh water brings in more dissolved air and minerals.

Why is boiler pressure too high?

1. Failed or waterlogged expansion tank

The expansion tank gives heated water room to expand. A diaphragm separates system water from an air charge. When the air charge is lost or the diaphragm fails, the tank fills with water and the pressure climbs every time the boiler heats, then falls when it cools. This is the most common cause.

  • Check: press the air valve on the tank briefly. Water coming out means the diaphragm has failed and the tank must be replaced.
  • Pre-charge: the air charge is checked with the tank isolated from the system and drained of pressure, and is set to the system's cold fill pressure, per the tank manufacturer's instructions.
  • Replacement: size the tank by system volume and temperature. The Amtrol EX-15 Extrol is one residential example. See heating expansion tanks and what an expansion tank does.

2. Fill valve leaking through

If the pressure creeps up while the boiler is cold, the fill valve may be passing water from the house supply. Close the feed valve for a day. If the rise stops, repair or replace the fill valve, then reopen the feed. See boiler fill valves.

3. Overfilling

Holding the fast-fill lever too long leaves too much water in the system. With the system cold, drain a small amount from a boiler drain until the gauge shows the normal cold pressure.

4. Leak in an indirect water heater coil or tankless coil

Domestic water at house pressure can leak through a failed coil into the boiler loop. If the pressure rises with the feed valve closed, have the coil tested.

When to call a professional

  • The relief valve discharges. The system reached the valve's set pressure or the valve has failed. Turn the boiler off and have the cause found. Never plug or cap the valve or its discharge pipe, and never fit a relief valve with a higher pressure rating than the boiler's maximum working pressure. Replacement relief valves must match the boiler's pressure and BTU/h capacity.
  • The pressure keeps rising or falling after the checks above.
  • The expansion tank, fill valve or a radiator valve needs replacing and the system must be drained.
  • You have a steam system, or you are not sure which type you have.
  • The gauge reads zero, or does not move between cold and hot. The gauge may have failed.

Frequently asked questions

Should the heating be on or off when bleeding radiators?

Off, with the circulator stopped and the radiators cooled. Run it first only to find the cold spots.

Why does boiler pressure rise when the heating is on?

Water expands as it heats, so a rise of a few psi is normal. A rise toward 30 psi usually means the expansion tank has lost its air charge or failed.

Why does my boiler keep losing pressure?

Bleeding releases some pressure, which is normal. Continued loss means a leak or a dripping relief valve.
