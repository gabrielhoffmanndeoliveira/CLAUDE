To connect PEX to copper, use a transition fitting made for both materials. There are three common ways: solder a sweat x PEX adapter onto the copper and crimp, clamp or expand the PEX onto its barb; push both pipes into a push-to-connect coupling; or join a threaded copper adapter to a threaded PEX adapter. PEX and copper tube of the same nominal size have the same outside diameter, so 1/2 in. PEX connects to 1/2 in. copper and 3/4 in. to 3/4 in. The right method depends on the tools you have and whether an open flame is safe where you are working.

Check the pipe sizes and the PEX type first

  • Copper: Types K, L and M have the same outside diameter for a given nominal size. 1/2 in. copper tube measures 5/8 in. outside, and 3/4 in. measures 7/8 in.
  • PEX: read the print line on the tubing. It gives the size, the type (PEX-A, PEX-B or PEX-C) and the fitting standards the tubing is listed for, such as ASTM F1807, F2159 or F1960.

The fitting system has to match the tubing. Crimp and clamp fittings (F1807 and F2159) work on any PEX listed for them. Cold expansion fittings (F1960) are for tubing listed to F1960, which is mainly PEX-A. See PEX-A vs PEX-B for the differences.

Before you cut

  • Shut off the water at the main or the nearest stop, open a faucet below the work to drain the line, and have a bucket ready.
  • Check where the pipe goes. Do not use PEX within the distance from a water heater or flue that the tubing manufacturer and your plumbing code prohibit. The Uniform Plumbing Code does not allow PEX within the first 18 inches of piping connected to a water heater.
  • In many homes the metal water piping is bonded to the electrical system. Replacing a section of copper with PEX breaks that path. See "When to call a professional" below.

Method 1: Sweat (solder) adapter

A sweat adapter has a copper socket or a fitting-size copper end on one side and a PEX barb or expansion end on the other.

  1. Cut the copper square with a tubing cutter. Deburr it, then clean the pipe end and the fitting socket with abrasive cloth or a fitting brush until bright.
  2. Solder the adapter with no PEX attached. Apply flux, assemble, heat the fitting evenly and feed lead-free solder into the joint. The line must be empty: a joint with water in it will not reach soldering temperature.
  3. Let the joint cool completely. Heat travels along copper and will damage PEX that is attached too soon.
  4. Attach the PEX. Cut the tubing square. For crimp or clamp: slide the ring on, push the tubing fully onto the barb, position the ring 1/8 to 1/4 in. from the end of the tubing and compress it with the tool. Check copper crimp rings with a go/no-go gauge. For expansion: expand the tubing and ring, push onto the fitting to the shoulder and let it shrink.
  5. Turn the water on slowly and check for leaks.

Where possible, solder the adapter to a short length of copper at a bench, then join that piece to the existing pipe with a coupling. This keeps the flame away from framing. When soldering in place, use a heat shield, keep an extinguisher within reach and check for smoldering afterward. Fittings are in copper sweat fittings and PEX crimp fittings (F1807).

Method 2: Push-to-connect fitting

A push-to-connect coupling holds each pipe with a stainless steel grab ring and seals it with an O-ring. Fittings certified to ASSE 1061 are made for copper, PEX and CPVC of copper tube size.

  1. Cut both pipes square. Use a tubing cutter on copper and a PEX cutter on PEX.
  2. Deburr and clean the copper. A burr or sharp edge can cut the O-ring. The sealing area must be free of scratches, solder and flat spots.
  3. Check for the tube stiffener. PEX needs a stiffener inside the tube end. Many brass push fittings have one built in. If not, fit one as the instructions require.
  4. Mark the insertion depth on each pipe using the manufacturer's depth gauge.
  5. Push each pipe in to the mark, then turn the water on and check for leaks.

Whether a push fitting may be concealed in a wall or buried depends on its listing and your local code, so check both. Browse push fittings.

Method 3: Threaded or press transition

Threaded: solder or press a male or female threaded adapter onto the copper, then screw on the matching PEX adapter (crimp, clamp or expansion x thread). Apply PTFE tape or pipe sealant rated for potable water to the male threads. Make the soldered joint first and let it cool before threading on a PEX adapter that already has tubing attached.

Press: with a copper press tool, a press x PEX adapter joins the two without flame and works on a line that will not drain fully. The copper must be clean, round and deburred, and marked for insertion depth.

For PEX-A expansion systems, see F1960 expansion fittings. Cutters, crimpers and clamp tools are in PEX tools, and tubing is in PEX pipe and tubing.

What mistakes cause leaks?

  • Soldering with the PEX attached, or attaching it before the joint has cooled.
  • Skipping the deburring. Burrs cut O-rings in push fittings.
  • Mixing fitting systems. Use rings and fittings listed for the tubing. Do not use F1960 expansion fittings on tubing that is not listed for F1960.
  • Tubing not fully inserted, or a crimp ring placed too close to or too far from the end.
  • No support. Fasten both the copper and the PEX near the transition so the fitting does not carry the weight or movement of the pipe.
  • Joining dissimilar metals directly. PEX adapters are brass or copper and are compatible with copper tube. Galvanized steel is a different case and needs a dielectric fitting.

When to call a professional

  • Electrical bonding. If the copper you are cutting is near the water meter or main shut-off, or a ground clamp is attached to the piping, have an electrician check whether a bonding jumper is required before the copper path is interrupted.
  • Water heater connections, especially on gas heaters, where clearances from the flue apply.
  • Soldering inside walls or near insulation if you have not soldered before. A press or push fitting, or a plumber, avoids the fire risk.
  • Work that needs a permit. Repiping and new branch lines usually do. Pressure test before closing walls.
  • Hydronic heating lines. These need oxygen-barrier PEX and components rated for the system temperature.

Frequently asked questions

Can you connect PEX directly to copper?

Yes, with a transition fitting: a sweat x PEX adapter, a push-to-connect coupling, or threaded or press adapters. PEX cannot be soldered or glued.

Is 1/2 in. PEX the same size as 1/2 in. copper?

The outside diameter is the same, 5/8 in., because both are copper tube size. The PEX wall is thicker, so its inside diameter is smaller.

Can PEX-A and PEX-B use the same copper adapters?

Crimp and clamp adapters fit any PEX listed for F1807 or F2159. Expansion adapters fit only tubing listed for F1960.
