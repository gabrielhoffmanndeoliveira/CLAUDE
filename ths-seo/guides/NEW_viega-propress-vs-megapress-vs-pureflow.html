The three Viega press systems are divided by pipe material. ProPress joins copper tube (and, in its stainless version, stainless steel tube). MegaPress joins iron pipe size carbon steel pipe, the black iron pipe used for hydronic heating, sprinklers and gas. PureFlow joins PEX tubing. The fittings, the jaws that press them and the sealing elements inside them are specific to each system, and within ProPress and MegaPress the sealing element decides whether a fitting may be used for water, gas or other fluids. Choosing correctly means matching three things: the pipe, the fluid and the tool.

ProPress: copper tube

ProPress fittings slide over copper tube and are pressed with a jaw or ring that deforms the fitting around the tube and compresses the sealing element in its raised bead. Viega's instructions limit ProPress to copper tube made to ASTM B88 (and B75 under additional conditions). Types K, L and M are all acceptable. Hard-drawn tube can be pressed from 1/2 to 4 inch. Annealed (soft) tube is limited to 1/2 through 1-1/4 inch.

The standard sealing element is EPDM, which Viega describes as suitable for water applications including hot water, and for compressed air. Viega lists an operating temperature range of 0°F to 250°F for the system. Viega also makes ProPress fittings in stainless steel for stainless tube, and a copper gas version with an HNBR element for jurisdictions and gas supplies where copper gas tubing is allowed.

Installation points from Viega's instructions:

  1. Cut the tube square and deburr inside and outside. A burr can cut the sealing element.
  2. Check that the sealing element is in place and undamaged. Do not oil or grease it.
  3. Mark the insertion depth on the tube (for example 3/4 inch for 1/2 inch tube and 7/8 inch for 3/4 and 1 inch tube), then push the fitting on to the mark.
  4. Press with the correct jaw, square to the tube, and let the tool complete its cycle.
  5. Pressure test. Viega allows testing up to 200 psi with air or 600 psi with water once the initial leak check is passed.

MegaPress: black iron and carbon steel pipe

MegaPress does for threaded steel pipe what ProPress does for soldered copper: it removes threading, welding and hot work permits. The fittings are carbon steel with a zinc-nickel coating and contain a sealing element plus a stainless steel grip ring that bites into the pipe. Viega's literature lists ASTM A53, A106, A135 and A795 carbon steel pipe from Schedule 5 to Schedule 40.

The pipe end matters more than with copper. It has to be clean, free of loose scale, paint, deep scratches and thread, for the full insertion depth, because the element seals on the outside of the pipe.

MegaPress or MegaPressG?

  • MegaPress with an EPDM element is for water-based systems such as hydronic heating, chilled water and fire sprinkler. An FKM element version covers higher temperatures and certain industrial fluids.
  • MegaPressG has an HNBR element and is the version for fuel gas (natural gas and propane) and fuel oil. Viega's specifications give a maximum of 125 psi for fuel gas, a temperature range of -40°F to 180°F, and conformance with ANSI LC4/CSA 6.32 for press-connect fittings in fuel gas systems. Viega's current fuel gas specification calls for Schedule 10 to Schedule 40 pipe.

Carbon steel MegaPress fittings are not for drinking water. Steel pipe and fittings corrode in oxygenated potable water. For stainless steel pipe in iron pipe sizes, Viega makes MegaPress Stainless.

See Viega MegaPress fittings and press fittings for gas.

PureFlow: PEX tubing

PureFlow is Viega's PEX plumbing system: tubing, fittings and manifolds. PureFlow Press fittings come in polymer or lead-free bronze with a stainless steel sleeve already attached. The tubing is pushed over the barb until it shows in the sleeve's view holes, and the sleeve is pressed with a PureFlow hand tool or a power tool with PureFlow jaws. There is no elastomer seal; the PEX itself seals against the barb.

Viega also makes PureFlow Crimp fittings, which use separate copper crimp rings and a crimp tool. Press and crimp parts are not interchangeable: a crimp fitting is not pressed and a press sleeve is not crimped. PureFlow tubing for drinking water is non-barrier PEX. For closed-loop heating with iron or steel components, use Viega's oxygen barrier tubing; see oxygen barrier vs non-barrier PEX.

See Viega PureFlow Press fittings and PureFlow Crimp fittings.

Sealing elements and the colored dot

On ProPress and MegaPress, a colored dot on the fitting identifies the sealing element:

Dot colorElementIntended for
GreenEPDMWater applications, including hot water; compressed air
WhiteFKMHigher temperatures and harsher industrial fluids
YellowHNBRFuel gas and fuel oil

Viega states that sealing elements are factory installed and are not meant to be swapped in the field as a way of converting a fitting. On PureFlow fittings, which have no sealing element, the green dot only indicates Smart Connect.

Smart Connect is the feature shared by all three systems: a fitting that has been assembled but not pressed is designed to leak visibly during the pressure test, so a missed joint is found before the wall is closed. It is a check on missed presses, not a substitute for testing to the pressure the code and the manufacturer require.

Tools and jaws

  • Each system and size uses its own jaw or ring profile. A ProPress jaw does not press a MegaPress fitting, and neither presses PureFlow.
  • Smaller sizes are pressed with jaws. Larger sizes use press rings closed by an actuator, and 2-1/2 to 4 inch MegaPress uses rings with Viega's Press Booster.
  • Press tools need calibration at the interval the tool maker sets. An out-of-calibration tool can produce joints that look complete and are not.

Quick selection steps

  1. Identify the pipe: copper tube, steel pipe or PEX.
  2. Identify the fluid: drinking water, heating water, gas, air or something else.
  3. Pick the system for the pipe, then the sealing element for the fluid (green, white or yellow dot).
  4. Confirm the size is in range, especially for soft copper.
  5. Confirm you have the matching jaw or ring for that system and size.

When to call a professional

Press fittings shorten the work but do not change who is allowed to do it. Fuel gas piping must be installed, tested and inspected by a licensed professional, and a permit is required in nearly every jurisdiction; a gas leak can cause fire or explosion. Fire sprinkler piping is work for a licensed sprinkler contractor. For water and heating systems, press tools are expensive and need calibration, so many owners hire a plumber for the pressing even on projects they otherwise run themselves. Always confirm with the local authority that press-connect fittings are accepted for the intended use.
