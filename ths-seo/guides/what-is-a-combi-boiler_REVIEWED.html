A combi boiler (combination boiler) is a single gas appliance that heats a home through a hot water heating system and also heats domestic hot water on demand, with no storage tank. It replaces a separate boiler and water heater with one wall-hung unit. A combi suits homes with radiators, baseboard or radiant floors and moderate hot water use. It is not the right choice where several showers run at once or where the home is heated by forced air.

How does a combi boiler work?

One cabinet holds a gas burner, a primary heat exchanger, a circulator, a three-way (diverter) valve, a plate heat exchanger for domestic water and the controls. The boiler works in one of two modes:

  1. Space heating. A thermostat calls for heat. The burner fires and the pump sends heated boiler water around the radiators, baseboard or floor loops. The burner modulates its output to match the load.
  2. Domestic hot water. A hot tap opens and a flow sensor detects it. The three-way valve diverts boiler water through the plate heat exchanger, where it heats the cold water passing through the other side. The burner goes to the output needed for that flow.

Domestic hot water has priority. While a tap is running, the heating circuit gets no heat. In a correctly sized system this is rarely noticed, because radiators and floors hold heat for the length of a shower. The boiler water and the tap water never mix.

If the house has forced air heat, a tankless water heater is the matching product, and how a tankless water heater works explains the domestic side. If the house has a boiler, the choice is between a combi and a heating boiler with an indirect water heater.

What size combi boiler do you need?

A combi has two ratings and both must fit. Most sizing errors come from checking only one.

Heating: BTU/h against the heat loss

The heating output should match the home's design heat loss from a room-by-room calculation such as ACCA Manual J. The rating plate of the old boiler is not a reliable guide, because many older boilers were oversized. The minimum firing rate matters as much as the maximum: a low minimum lets the boiler run long, steady cycles in mild weather. The Navien NCB-240/130H, for example, lists a heating input range of 13,000 to 130,000 BTU/h, and the IBC CX199 has a 10:1 turndown from 199,000 BTU/h.

Hot water: GPM at your temperature rise

The domestic side is rated in gallons per minute (GPM) at a stated temperature rise. Temperature rise is the hot water setpoint minus the incoming cold water temperature: 120°F minus 50°F is a 70°F rise. The relationship is:

GPM = BTU/h output ÷ (500 × temperature rise in °F)

Navien lists the NCB-240/130H at 5.1 GPM at a 70°F rise from a 199,900 BTU/h domestic input. Add up the fixtures that run at the same time and compare. The flow rate is marked on most showerheads and faucets. In cold climates the winter rise is larger, so the same boiler delivers fewer gallons per minute.

When is a combi boiler a good fit?

  • The home has hydronic heat and one or two bathrooms, and rarely runs more than two hot fixtures at once.
  • Mechanical room space is limited.
  • The heat loss is moderate and falls inside the boiler's modulation range.

When is a boiler with an indirect tank the better choice?

  • Several showers, a large soaking tub or a multi-head shower run at the same time.
  • The incoming water is very cold or very hard. Hard water scales a plate heat exchanger.
  • The heating load is large and you want to size the boiler to the heat loss alone.
  • You want domestic hot water recirculation, which a storage tank handles more simply.

For that route, see condensing boilers and indirect water heaters.

What does a combi boiler installation need?

  • Venting. Condensing combis produce cool, acidic flue gas and vent through PVC, CPVC, polypropylene or stainless steel, never into an unlined masonry chimney. The approved materials, diameters and maximum lengths are specific to each model, and each elbow reduces the allowed length.
  • Condensate drain. The condensate is acidic and must run to a drain, through a condensate neutralizer where the manual or local code requires one.
  • Gas supply. A combi fires at a much higher rate for hot water than for heating (199,900 BTU/h on the NCB-240/130H), often well above the input of the boiler it replaces. The gas pipe, meter and regulator must be sized for the total connected load and pipe length. An undersized line causes ignition faults and poor hot water output.
  • Hydronic components. The heating loop is closed and pressurized. It needs a pressure relief valve, a fill valve with backflow protection, air removal and expansion capacity. Some combis have an internal expansion vessel that is too small for a large system, so an external heating expansion tank may be required.
  • Service valves. Isolation valves on the domestic side allow the plate heat exchanger to be descaled.

What maintenance does a combi boiler need?

  • Check system pressure on the gauge or display with the system cold and compare it with the manual's range. Frequent pressure loss means a leak. See how to bleed a radiator and fix high boiler pressure.
  • Descale the domestic heat exchanger on the schedule in the manual, more often in hard water.
  • Clean the filters on the cold water inlet and the heating return.
  • Keep the condensate trap clean and the vent and intake terminals clear of debris and snow.
  • Yearly professional service: burner inspection, combustion analysis, relief valve check and expansion tank pre-charge.

When to call a professional

Installing a combi boiler is licensed work: it combines gas piping, venting, condensate, hydronic piping, potable water and electrical connections, and it needs a permit in most jurisdictions. A combustion analysis at startup is the only way to confirm the boiler is burning safely. Also call a professional when:

  • The relief valve discharges or the pressure climbs toward its setting. Never plug or cap a relief valve.
  • The boiler shows repeated ignition or flame loss codes.
  • You smell gas or flue gas. Leave the building and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Any work is needed inside the sealed combustion chamber or on the gas valve.

Homeowner tasks are limited to checking the pressure, keeping terminals clear and cleaning the filters the manual describes as user-serviceable.

Frequently asked questions

Is a combi boiler the same as a tankless water heater?

No. Both heat water on demand, but a combi also runs a hydronic heating system. A tankless water heater makes domestic hot water only.

Can a combi boiler run two showers at once?

Yes, if its GPM at your winter temperature rise covers both showerheads. For example, two 2.0 GPM showerheads need about 4 GPM of mixed water, slightly less from the boiler.

Does a combi boiler need an expansion tank?

The heating loop always needs expansion capacity. Check whether the model has an internal vessel and whether it is large enough for the system volume.
