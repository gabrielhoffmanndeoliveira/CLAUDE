Navien vs Rinnai comes down to installation details more than brand. Both make condensing gas tankless water heaters rated up to about 199,000 BTU/h with efficiency ratings of 0.95 to 0.98 UEF, and both offer models with a built-in recirculation pump. The practical differences are venting options, how each brand handles recirculation, the non-condensing lines, and which brand your installer is trained on. This guide compares the two on the facts each manufacturer publishes. We sell Navien, so use the manufacturer documents linked from each product page to check anything that matters for your job.

Which models are you actually comparing?

Navien

  • NPE-S2: the standard condensing model, such as the NPE-240S2 (199,900 BTU/h).
  • NPE-A2: the same heater with ComfortFlow, a built-in recirculation pump and buffer tank, such as the NPE-240A2.
  • NHW-A and NHW-S: non-condensing lines rated 0.81 UEF. The NHW-AI is an indoor model with a built-in pump and buffer tank, and the NHW-SU installs indoors or outdoors.

Rinnai

  • SENSEI RX: the residential condensing line. Models with a P in the name (RXP) include a recirculation pump.
  • RE series: the non-condensing line. REP models include a pump.

How does the efficiency compare?

UEF (Uniform Energy Factor) is the U.S. Department of Energy rating for water heaters. Navien publishes 0.96 UEF for the NPE-S2 and 0.95 for the NPE-A2. Rinnai publishes 0.98 UEF for models such as the RXP199iN. A difference of two or three hundredths changes the yearly gas bill only slightly. Correct sizing, an adequate gas supply and regular descaling affect real performance more. Check the exact model in the manufacturer's specification sheet or the ENERGY STAR product list.

How does the venting compare?

Venting often decides the installation cost, so price both brands against your real vent route.

  • Navien NPE-2: Navien lists 2 in. venting up to 75 ft and 3 in. venting up to 150 ft, with deductions for each elbow. Approved materials include PVC, CPVC, polypropylene and stainless steel.
  • Navien NHW: 3 in. x 5 in. concentric vent.
  • Rinnai SENSEI RX: Rinnai's specification sheet lists concentric venting or Schedule 40 PVC/CPVC, as direct vent (concentric or twin pipe) or room air. Diameters and maximum lengths are in the installation manual for the model.
  • Rinnai RE: concentric vent. Confirm the size in the manual.

Vent pipe from a tank water heater cannot be reused for either brand. If you are replacing an existing tankless unit of the same brand and type, the existing vent may be reusable only if the new model's manual approves that vent system.

How does recirculation compare?

Recirculation keeps hot water near the fixtures so the wait at the tap is shorter.

  • Navien ComfortFlow (NPE-A2, NHW-AI): a built-in pump and a small buffer tank. The buffer tank reduces the short burst of cool water that can occur when a tankless heater restarts between draws. The manual covers piping with a dedicated return line and without one.
  • Rinnai (RXP, REP): a built-in pump. Rinnai calls its scheduling feature Smart-Circ, which sets recirculation times from the household's hot water use.

With either brand, a house without a dedicated return line needs a thermostatic crossover valve at the farthest fixture. That method sends some warm water into the cold line while it runs.

What matters more than the brand?

  • Sizing. Size by flow rate at your winter temperature rise, not by BTU alone. See what size tankless water heater you need.
  • Gas supply. A 199,000 BTU/h heater often needs more gas than the tank it replaces. The pipe, meter and regulator must be checked against the total connected load.
  • Water quality. Both brands require periodic descaling in hard water. Isolation valves from a tankless valve kit make that possible without cutting pipe.
  • Condensate. Condensing models need a drain and, where required, a condensate neutralizer.
  • Service. Ask which brand your installer stocks parts for and is trained to commission.

Which should you buy?

  • Navien NPE-A2: you want recirculation with a buffer tank built into the heater.
  • Navien NPE-S2: you want a condensing unit without recirculation and need 2 in. vent runs up to 75 ft.
  • Rinnai RX or RXP: you are replacing a Rinnai, your installer works mainly with Rinnai, or a specific RX model meets your efficiency target.
  • Non-condensing (Navien NHW or Rinnai RE): you are replacing a non-condensing unit, or the heater goes outdoors and a condensate drain is impractical.

When to call a professional

Installing or converting a gas tankless water heater is not a homeowner job in most jurisdictions. It involves gas piping, venting, a condensate drain, a 120 V supply and combustion setup, and it usually needs a permit. Use a licensed installer for the installation, for any propane conversion, for gas pressure checks and for any work inside the sealed combustion chamber. Both manufacturers also tie their warranty terms to installation according to the manual. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside.

Frequently asked questions

Is Navien or Rinnai more efficient?

On paper, Rinnai's highest RX rating (0.98 UEF) is slightly above Navien's NPE-S2 (0.96 UEF). The difference in gas use is small.

Can both brands vent with PVC?

The condensing lines can, within the limits in each installation manual. Non-condensing models from either brand need the manufacturer's concentric or stainless vent.

Do I need a recirculation model?

Only if long pipe runs make you wait for hot water. In a compact house with short runs, a standard model is simpler and has fewer parts.
