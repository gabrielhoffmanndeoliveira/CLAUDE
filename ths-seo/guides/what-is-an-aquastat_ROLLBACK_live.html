If you have a hot water boiler, there is a good chance a small gray box on the side of it decides when the burner fires and when the circulator runs. That box is the aquastat. When it fails or is set wrong, the boiler may not turn on, may short-cycle or may run too hot. This guide explains what an aquastat does, the main types (including the Honeywell/Resideo L4006, L8148 and L8124), how to read the settings, and how to choose a replacement.

Safety first: many aquastats and aquastat relays switch 120-volt power to the burner and circulator. Turn off the boiler's service switch and confirm the power is off before you open a cover. Boiler controls are safety devices; if you are not comfortable with line-voltage wiring, have a qualified heating technician do the work.

What is an aquastat?

An aquastat is a temperature-actuated switch for water. A sensing bulb sits in the boiler water, usually inside a brass or copper immersion well, and a switch inside the case opens or closes when the water reaches the set temperature. In a hot water heating system, aquastats do up to three jobs:

  • High limit: shuts the burner off when the boiler water reaches the maximum setting, so the boiler cannot overheat.
  • Low limit: keeps the boiler water at a minimum temperature, often to supply a tankless domestic hot water coil or to limit condensation in a cast iron boiler.
  • Circulator control: holds the circulator off until the water is hot enough, so cold water is not pushed into the system.

The differential is the temperature swing between the switch opening and closing. With a 180°F high limit and a 15°F differential, the burner shuts off at 180°F and is allowed to fire again near 165°F. A wider differential means longer, less frequent burns; a narrow one holds temperature more tightly.

Types of aquastats

Single-function immersion aquastats (L4006, L6006)

These are the simplest controls: one switch, one setting. Two terms matter when you read the label:

  • Breaks on rise means the switch opens as the temperature rises. That is how a high limit works, as on the L4006A1017 (100 to 240°F, adjustable 5 to 30°F differential).
  • Makes on rise means the switch closes as the temperature rises, the action used to start a circulator only once the water is hot, as on the L4006B1163.

Models with SPDT contacts, such as the L6006A1244, can be wired either way.

Manual reset high limits (L4006E, L4008E)

A manual reset aquastat is a backup safety limit. If the water ever reaches its setting, it locks out the burner and stays off until someone presses the reset button. Examples include the L4006E1000 (supplied with a 1/2 inch well) and the remote-bulb L4008E1156. A manual reset that keeps tripping is a warning sign: something is letting the boiler overheat, and the cause should be found before the boiler goes back into service.

Surface mount and remote bulb aquastats

Strap-on (surface mount) models, such as the L6006C1018, clamp to a pipe instead of using a well. Remote bulb models in the L4008 family have a capillary tube so the case can be mounted away from the sensing point.

Aquastat relays (L8148)

An aquastat relay combines a high limit with a relay that runs the burner and circulator from a low-voltage thermostat. The thermostat connects to the relay's T T terminals, and the control switches 120-volt power to the burner and pump. Resideo's L8148 relays, including the L8148A1017 (140 to 240°F, fixed 8°F differential) and the 180 to 240°F L8148E1265, are common on gas boilers. The L8148J1009 is a universal-mount version.

Triple aquastats (L8124)

A triple aquastat adds a low limit to the high limit and circulator functions. That is the classic control on oil boilers with a tankless coil, where the boiler stays warm all year to make domestic hot water. The L8124A1007 has a 130 to 240°F high limit with a fixed 10°F differential and a 110 to 220°F low limit with a 10 to 25°F adjustable differential. Multizone systems add a separate circulator and an R845A relay for each zone.

Electronic aquastats (L7224U)

The L7224U1002 universal electronic oil aquastat replaces the mechanical high limit and circulator controls on an oil-fired boiler with one electronic control. Instead of holding one high limit all winter, it can lower the boiler target when the heating load is light and raise it when the house calls hard. It uses a plug-in sensor, such as the 50001464-001 sensor assembly.

Boiler won't turn on? Check the aquastat

When a hot water boiler will not fire on the first cold night, the aquastat is one of the first places to look:

  1. Power and thermostat. Make sure the service switch is on, the fuse or breaker is good and the thermostat is calling for heat. A low-voltage thermostat on an aquastat relay connects to T T; jumping those terminals briefly is a common technician test.
  2. Manual reset. Look for a tripped reset button on any manual reset limit, and on the oil burner primary control. Press it once only. If it trips again, call a technician.
  3. Settings. Someone may have turned the high limit down. A setting below the water temperature keeps the burner off.
  4. Relay and transformer. On an L8148 or L8124, a failed transformer or relay can stop both the burner and the circulator.
  5. Not the aquastat? If the burner fires but the radiators stay cold, look at the circulator and air in the system. See how to choose a boiler circulator and how to bleed radiators and fix boiler pressure.

If your system has zone valves rather than zone pumps, also check the end switches; our guide to zone valve replacement covers them.

How to set a boiler aquastat

Always start with the boiler manufacturer's recommended settings. As general guidance:

  • High limit: set it high enough to heat the house on the coldest days but no higher than the boiler and system allow. Many baseboard systems run around 180°F; radiant floors run much cooler and usually need a mixing device. See our mixing valve guide.
  • Low limit: must be set below the high limit, with at least the gap the control's instructions call for. If you don't have a tankless coil, ask your technician whether the low limit can be turned down or off to save fuel, within the boiler maker's minimum water temperature.
  • Differential: a larger differential reduces short cycling; a smaller one gives steadier temperatures.

How to choose a replacement aquastat

Match the control coming out, not just the boiler nameplate. Check:

  1. Function: single limit, aquastat relay, triple aquastat or electronic.
  2. Switch action: breaks on rise, makes on rise or SPDT.
  3. Range and differential: for example 100 to 240°F with an adjustable differential, or a fixed differential.
  4. Reset type: automatic or manual reset.
  5. Mounting: immersion well, strap-on or remote bulb. If the old well is reused, check its thread size and insertion depth. Replacement wells include the 121371A (1/2 inch NPT) and 121371B (3/4 inch NPT).
  6. Voltage and wiring: 120V line voltage, 24V or millivolt thermostat circuits.

Coat the sensing bulb with heat conductive grease before sliding it into the well so it reads the water temperature accurately.

Frequently asked questions

What does an aquastat do on a boiler?

It senses the boiler water temperature and switches the burner, the circulator or both, so the water stays within its working range and never exceeds the high limit.

What is the difference between an aquastat and a thermostat?

A room thermostat senses air temperature and calls for heat. An aquastat senses water temperature and decides whether the boiler can fire and the pump can run.

What should a boiler aquastat be set to?

Use the boiler manufacturer's settings. Many baseboard systems run a high limit around 180°F, with the low limit set lower by at least the gap the control requires.

Can I replace an L8148 with an L8124?

Not directly. They have different functions and wiring. Replace with the same type unless a technician confirms the system needs the extra low limit function.
