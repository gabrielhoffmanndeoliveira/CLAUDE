To replace a discontinued Honeywell thermostat, you need three facts about the old one: the system type it controls (conventional furnace or boiler, heat pump, or line voltage), the number of heating and cooling stages, and whether it was programmable. The model number printed inside the old thermostat gives most of that. With those facts, the current Honeywell Home lines made by Resideo cover nearly every case: FocusPRO N100 for non-programmable, FocusPRO P200 for programmable, and T6 Pro for programmable models with more installer options and smart versions. The replacement must support at least as many stages as the equipment has.

Step 1: Find the full model number

Pull the thermostat body off its wall plate. On most Honeywell models it lifts or unclips straight off. The model number is on a label on the back of the body, and it looks like TH5110D1006 or TH6220D1028. The name on the front (FocusPRO, VisionPRO, PRO 3000) is the family; the label gives the exact model. Note the full string, including the letter and the last four digits.

Before removing anything else, switch off power to the heating and cooling equipment and take a photo of the wires on their terminals.

Step 2: Read the model number

Honeywell low-voltage wall thermostats in the TH range follow a consistent pattern. Taking TH6220D1028 as the example:

PartExampleWhat it indicates
PrefixTHLow-voltage wall thermostat
First digit6Series. On older models: 3 = PRO 3000, 4 = PRO 4000, 5 = FocusPRO 5000, 6 = FocusPRO 6000, 8 = VisionPRO 8000
Second digit2Maximum heating stages
Third digit2Maximum cooling stages
LettersDVariant. WF marks Wi-Fi, ZW marks Z-Wave, R marks RedLINK wireless
Last four digits1028Specific version: display size, color, packaging

So a TH5110D is a FocusPRO 5000 for one heat and one cool stage, a TH6220D is a FocusPRO 6000 for two heat and two cool, and a TH8320 is a VisionPRO 8000 for three heat and two cool. Resideo does not publish this decoding as a formal key, so treat it as a quick read and confirm the stages against the product name on the label or the manual for that model.

The same pattern holds on the current lines: TH6210U is a T6 Pro for up to 2 heat/1 cool, TH6220U for 2 heat/2 cool and TH6320U for 3 heat/2 cool.

Step 3: Confirm the stages from the wiring

The model number tells you what the old thermostat could do. The wires tell you what the equipment actually uses.

TerminalFunction
R, Rc, Rh24 V power from the transformer
CCommon, for powering the thermostat
W or W1First stage heat
W2Second stage heat. On a heat pump, Aux or E is auxiliary or emergency heat
Y or Y1First stage cooling (compressor)
Y2Second stage cooling

O/BHeat pump reversing valve. If this wire is present, the system is a heat pump

Stage counts are stated differently for the two system types. "2 Heat/1 Cool heat pump" means one compressor stage plus auxiliary heat. "1 Heat/1 Cool conventional" means a single-stage furnace or boiler with single-stage cooling. Many current models list both, for example "up to 2 Heat/1 Cool heat pump, 1 Heat/1 Cool conventional."

Stop if the wires are thick and connected with wire nuts to a thermostat mounted on an electrical box. That is a line voltage thermostat (120 or 240 V) for electric baseboard or similar heat. None of the low-voltage models in this guide can replace it. See line voltage thermostats instead.

Rules for choosing

  • Equal or more stages is fine. A 2H/2C thermostat can run a 1H/1C system; it is configured for the equipment during setup. Fewer stages is not acceptable: a second stage left unconnected never runs.
  • Heat pumps need a heat pump capable model. Confirm that it supports auxiliary heat if you have it, and dual fuel if the backup is a gas furnace.
  • Programmable can replace non-programmable, and schedules can be switched off in setup on most models.
  • Hydronic radiant floors with a floor sensor need the hydronic T6 Pro, not a standard model.
  • Wireless (RedLINK) models work with matching equipment interface modules. Replace them with a compatible RedLINK thermostat, not a standard wired one, unless the wiring is changed.

Examples: FocusPRO N100 TH1210U4001, FocusPRO P200 TH2210U4005, T6 Pro TH6210U2001, T6 Pro TH6220U2000 and T6 Pro TH6320U2008. The full range is in thermostats and Honeywell Pro series thermostats.

Step 5: Check power and the wall plate

  • Power. Many non-connected models run on batteries, on a C wire, or both. Wi-Fi models generally need a C wire or an adapter. If you have no C wire, see do you need a C-wire.
  • Wall plate. The T6 Pro mounts on Resideo's UWP wall plate, which is different from the FocusPRO 5000 and 6000 sub-base. Whichever model you choose, plan to move the wires to the new plate. A cover plate hides the outline left by a larger old thermostat; see thermostat accessories.
  • Installer setup. After wiring, the thermostat must be told the system type and number of stages in its installer menu. A heat pump set up as conventional, or the reverse, will run the wrong equipment.

For the wiring procedure itself, see how to install a thermostat.

When to call a professional

Swapping a low-voltage thermostat like-for-like is a common homeowner job with the power off. Call an HVAC technician if the wiring does not match any diagram in the new manual, if the system is dual fuel, multi-stage with accessories (humidifier, ventilation), zoned through a zone panel, or wireless, if a C wire has to be added at the furnace or air handler, or if a fuse on the control board blows after the swap. Line voltage thermostats carry 120 or 240 V; that work belongs to a licensed electrician unless you are qualified and the circuit is switched off and verified dead.
