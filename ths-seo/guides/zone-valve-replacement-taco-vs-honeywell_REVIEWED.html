Most zone valve replacements do not require replacing the whole valve. On Taco 570 series and Honeywell V8043 zone valves, the part that usually fails is the power head (actuator), and it can be changed without draining the system or cutting pipe. Replace the head with the same brand, series and voltage, and wire the new one exactly like the old one. The full valve needs replacing only when the body leaks, is seized, or you are changing brand or pipe connection. This guide explains how to tell which part has failed, how Taco and Honeywell (Resideo) valves differ, and what each replacement involves.

Both brands have a manual opening lever on the valve. If the lever moves the valve open and the zone then heats, the body is working and the head or its wiring is at fault.

How does a zone valve work?

A zone valve is an electrically operated valve on the piping of one heating zone. When that zone's thermostat calls for heat, 24 VAC from a transformer powers the actuator and the valve opens. When the valve is fully open, an end switch inside the head closes and signals the boiler and circulator to run. When the thermostat is satisfied, power is removed and the valve closes.

The valve has two parts: the brass body, which is soldered, threaded or pressed into the piping, and the power head, which holds the motor and end switch.

Neither design is better in every system. The practical rule is to replace like with like, so the wiring, transformer load and piping stay the same. Heads are not interchangeable between brands, and not always between series of the same brand: Taco Zone Sentry valves, for example, do not use 570 series heads.

Which wiring type do you have?

  • Two-wire, no end switch: the thermostat circuit powers the actuator and nothing else. Used where a zone control panel starts the boiler and circulator.
  • Three-wire (Taco 570): three terminals serve both the motor and the end switch.
  • Four-wire (Honeywell V8043 models with an end switch): two motor connections and two end switch connections.

Because the two brands arrange the motor and end switch connections differently, they cannot be swapped wire for wire. Converting between them means rewiring to the new valve's diagram and checking that the transformer can carry the load of all the valves.

Which models are common?

  • Taco 571-2: the Taco 571-2 is a 3/4 in. sweat, two-way heat motor zone valve. The 570 family covers other pipe sizes.
  • Taco 555-050RP: the 555-050RP power unit is a Taco replacement power head. Confirm on Taco's replacement parts list that it fits your valve model before ordering.
  • Honeywell V8043E1012: the V8043E1012/U is a 24 VAC, 3/4 in. sweat, normally closed valve with 18 in. lead wires.
  • Honeywell V8044: the three-way diverting version of the V8043.

How to replace a zone valve power head

This is low-voltage work with the system left full. It still requires the power to be off, because zone valve wiring connects to boiler controls.

  1. Copy the full model number from the head and the valve body, and order the head listed for that valve.
  2. Turn off power to the boiler and to the zone valve transformer or zone control.
  3. Photograph the wiring and label each wire.
  4. Disconnect the wires.
  5. Remove the head from the body as the manufacturer's instructions describe for that model. Do not loosen the body or its bonnet: the system is under pressure.
  6. Fit the new head and check that it is fully seated and latched.
  7. Reconnect the wires to the same terminals or lead colors, following the diagram supplied with the head.
  8. Restore power and raise the zone thermostat. Confirm that the valve opens, the end switch starts the boiler or circulator, and the zone heats. Then lower the thermostat and confirm that the valve closes and the zone cools.

When does the whole valve need replacing?

  • The body or stem leaks.
  • The valve stays stuck open or closed with a working head.
  • You are changing brand, pipe size or connection type, or the head for that valve is no longer made.

Replacing the body means isolating or draining the zone, removing the old valve, soldering, threading or pressing in the new one with the flow arrow in the right direction, then refilling, purging air and checking the system pressure. Follow the manufacturer's soldering instructions for a sweat body, which may require the head to be removed first, so heat does not damage the valve. After refilling, see how to bleed a radiator and check boiler pressure.

What to confirm before you order

  • Brand, series and full model number of the head and the body
  • Voltage: 24 VAC or 120 V. Never fit a 24 V head on a 120 V circuit, or the reverse.
  • Normally closed or normally open
  • End switch or no end switch, and terminal block or lead wires
  • For a full valve: pipe size, connection type (sweat, threaded, flare or press) and two-way or three-way

When to call a professional

  • Full valve replacement, unless you are experienced with soldering and with draining, refilling and purging a hydronic system.
  • Any 120 V zone valve (V4043, V4044) or line voltage wiring.
  • A transformer or zone control that keeps failing, or a new head that burns out, which points to a wiring fault or an overloaded transformer.
  • Changing brands, where the end switch wiring to the boiler has to be redesigned.
  • The boiler does not fire after the repair. Do not jumper boiler controls or limits to test it.
  • The relief valve discharges or the pressure will not stabilize after refilling.

Frequently asked questions

Can I replace a Taco zone valve with a Honeywell?

Yes, but it is a full valve replacement and a rewiring job, because the end switch arrangements differ. Replacing with the same brand is simpler.

Do I have to drain the system to replace a zone valve head?

Usually not. On Taco 570 valves and on Honeywell V8043 valves with a removable head, the head comes off the body with the system full. Check the instructions for your model.

Why does a Taco zone valve take time to open?

Its heat motor has to warm up before it moves the valve. The delay is normal for that design.
