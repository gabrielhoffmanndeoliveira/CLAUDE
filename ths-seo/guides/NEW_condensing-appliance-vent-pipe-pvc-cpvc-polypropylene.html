A condensing boiler or condensing tankless water heater can only be vented with the materials its installation manual lists, and for most current models that list is solid-core Schedule 40 PVC, CPVC, polypropylene, or stainless steel. PVC is acceptable only where the manual allows it and flue gas stays within its temperature limit. CPVC handles more heat. Polypropylene handles the most of the three plastics and is the only one manufactured and listed specifically as a flue gas vent system. Metal B-vent and single-wall galvanized pipe, used on older atmospheric appliances, must never be used.

Why condensing appliances need different vent pipe

A condensing appliance takes so much heat out of the flue gas that water vapor in it condenses. The exhaust that leaves is cool, usually well under 200°F, and wet. Two things follow:

  • The condensate is acidic. It corrodes galvanized steel and the aluminum inner wall of B-vent.
  • The vent is under positive pressure. A fan pushes the exhaust out, so every joint has to be gas-tight. A leak inside the building releases flue gas, including carbon monoxide.

Appliances of this kind are classified as Category IV: positive vent pressure and condensing. Navien, for example, states that its boilers are Category IV and require a vent system approved for that category. The vent materials that qualify are corrosion-resistant plastics and special stainless steel.

The temperature figures are the limits used in the ULC S636 vent standard classes and quoted by vent manufacturers. The appliance manual always decides: if it says CPVC or polypropylene only, PVC is not an option, whatever the pipe's own rating.

Why polypropylene is increasingly specified

PVC and CPVC pipe are made to plumbing pressure-pipe standards. Pipe manufacturers generally do not rate them as flue gas vents; their use rests on the appliance manufacturer's instructions. Polypropylene vent systems are different: they are tested and listed as venting, under UL 1738 in the United States and ULC S636 in Canada. Canada has required S636-listed venting for years, and US codes and manufacturers have been moving toward listed vent systems as well. Centrotherm states that its polypropylene vent line is listed to both standards with a 230°F flue gas rating.

Practical differences on the job:

  • No cement, no cure time. Joints push together and are locked with a connector ring. The gasket must be seated and lubricated with the manufacturer's lubricant only.
  • Joints can be taken apart for service or inspection.
  • One system, one manufacturer. Polypropylene vent components are listed as a system. Do not mix pipe, elbows or terminations from different brands, and use the appliance adapter the boiler or water heater manufacturer specifies.
  • UV exposure. Standard gray polypropylene is not for outdoor exposure. Use the black UV-resistant end pipes and terminations made for the outside portion.

Vent length and diameter: worked example

Every manual gives a maximum equivalent length for each pipe diameter, and counts each elbow as a number of feet. Navien's specification sheet for the NPE-2 condensing tankless series gives:

Vent diameterMaximum equivalent lengthEach 90° elbow counts as
2 inch75 ft8 ft
3 inch150 ft5 ft

So a 2 inch vent with 30 feet of straight pipe and four elbows has an equivalent length of 30 + (4 × 8) = 62 feet, which is inside the limit. Add two more elbows and it reaches 78 feet, and the run has to go up to 3 inch. The same calculation applies separately to the air intake pipe on a two-pipe installation. Figures differ by model and by brand, so use the table in the manual for the unit you are installing.

Installation rules that apply to every material

  1. Slope the exhaust back to the appliance, typically 1/4 inch per foot, so condensate drains into the unit's trap instead of pooling in the pipe or dripping from the termination. Follow the manual if it specifies a different slope.
  2. Support the pipe at the spacing the vent or appliance manufacturer gives, so it cannot sag and hold water.
  3. Terminate with the required clearances from windows, doors, air inlets, gas meters, grade and snow level. These come from the manual and from the fuel gas code adopted locally (NFPA 54 / ANSI Z223.1 in most of the US).
  4. Keep intake and exhaust separated as the manual shows, or use the approved concentric termination.
  5. Do not connect to another appliance's vent. A Category IV appliance cannot share a vent with an atmospheric water heater or furnace. Multi-unit common venting is allowed only with the manufacturer's engineered kit, which includes backflow dampers.
  6. Do not use an unlined chimney as the vent. A chimney can serve as a chase for a listed liner, such as flexible polypropylene, if nothing else vents into it.
  7. Drain the condensate properly. Route it to a drain through a condensate neutralizer where required, and use a condensate pump if there is no gravity drain.

Replacing an older appliance

When a non-condensing boiler or atmospheric water heater is replaced with a condensing unit, the old metal vent cannot be reused. Plan a new route to a side wall or through the roof. If the old appliance shared a chimney with another one that stays in service, such as a water heater left behind after a boiler upgrade, that chimney may now be oversized for the remaining appliance and has to be checked and usually relined.

When to call a professional

Venting a gas appliance is combustion safety work. A wrong material, a joint that is not sealed, a vent that is too long or a termination in the wrong place can put carbon monoxide into the building or shut the appliance down on a fault. In most jurisdictions installing or replacing a gas boiler or water heater requires a permit and a licensed installer, and manufacturers generally require installation by a licensed professional for the warranty to apply. Use this guide to understand the materials and to order the right parts with your installer. Have the installer verify combustion with an analyzer at start-up, and keep working carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas and on each level of the home.
