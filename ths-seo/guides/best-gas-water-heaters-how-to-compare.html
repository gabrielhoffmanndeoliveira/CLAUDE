The best gas water heater for a home is the tank model that fits the existing vent, burns the fuel you have, and has a first-hour rating that covers your busiest hour of hot water use. Brand comes after those three. A heater with the wrong vent type can cost more to install than the heater itself, so compare models in the order below: vent type, fuel, first-hour rating, BTU input, efficiency (UEF), then size and features.

How to compare gas water heaters: the short checklist

  1. Vent type: the same as the heater you are replacing, unless you plan a vent change.
  2. Fuel: natural gas or propane, matched to your supply.
  3. First-hour rating (FHR): equal to or slightly above your peak-hour demand.
  4. BTU/h input: within what your gas line and meter can supply.
  5. UEF: the highest you can justify, compared within the same usage bin.
  6. Dimensions: height, diameter and clearances that fit the space.
  7. Local rules: low-NOx, seismic strapping, expansion tank and permit requirements.

Which vent type do you need?

Venting decides which models can be installed without rebuilding the flue. It is the first filter, before brand or capacity.

Replacing like with like is almost always the simplest job. Changing vent type means new vent pipe, new wall or roof penetrations and sometimes a new electrical circuit. Vent material, diameter and maximum length are set by each model's installation manual, not by a general rule.

Natural gas or propane?

Natural gas and propane models use different orifices and gas control settings. Buy the version built for your fuel. A heater can be converted only with the manufacturer's kit for that exact model, installed by a qualified technician, and many residential tanks have no approved kit. The fuel is printed on the rating plate and is part of the model number.

What first-hour rating do you need?

The first-hour rating is the number of gallons of hot water the heater can deliver in one hour, starting with a full hot tank. It is printed on the yellow EnergyGuide label. Tank size alone does not tell you this: two 50-gallon heaters with different burners have different first-hour ratings.

To estimate your peak-hour demand, list the hot water uses that happen in your busiest hour and add them up. The U.S. Department of Energy sizing worksheet uses about 20 gallons for a shower, 7 gallons for a dishwasher cycle and 25 gallons for a load of laundry. Two showers, a dishwasher cycle and a load of laundry in the same hour come to roughly 72 gallons, so look for a first-hour rating at or just above that number. How much BTU input is enough?

BTU/h input is the size of the burner. A higher input reheats the tank faster, which raises the first-hour rating without a larger tank. Residential gas tanks are built with inputs from about 30,000 BTU/h to 75,000 BTU/h; above 75,000 BTU/h a heater is classed as commercial under federal efficiency rules.

A larger burner needs more gas. Before choosing a high-input model, have your installer confirm that the gas pipe, the meter and the regulator can carry the heater's full input together with every other gas appliance in the house. An undersized gas line causes poor ignition and a burner that never reaches its rating.

How do you compare efficiency (UEF)?

Uniform Energy Factor (UEF) is the federal efficiency rating for water heaters. A higher number means more of the fuel's energy ends up in the water. UEF is tested in four draw patterns (very small, low, medium and high usage), and a rating is comparable only with other heaters tested in the same pattern. The pattern and the estimated yearly operating cost are both on the EnergyGuide label.

Standard atmospheric tanks sit at the lower end of the range. Powered damper, power vent and condensing designs rate higher because less heat escapes through the flue.

What else should you check before ordering?

  • Height and diameter. Short and tall versions of the same capacity exist for low ceilings and narrow closets. Measure the space, the doorway and the height of the existing vent connection.
  • Low-NOx requirements. Some air quality districts, mainly in California, require ultra-low-NOx burners. Check the local rule before ordering.
  • Relief valve location. The temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve may be on the top or the side. Matching the old position simplifies the discharge piping.
  • Thermal expansion. Where the cold supply has a check valve, backflow preventer or pressure reducing valve, plumbing codes require thermal expansion control, usually a water heater expansion tank.
  • Installation parts. Plan for a new gas connector and sediment trap, water heater connectors, a drain pan, seismic straps where required and a shut-off valve. See water heater gas supplies and shut-off valves and leak protection.

When to call a professional

Installing a gas water heater involves gas piping, combustion air, venting and a T&P relief valve. Errors in any of them can cause a gas leak, carbon monoxide in the house or a scalding discharge. In most jurisdictions the work needs a permit and a licensed plumber or gas fitter. Hire a professional when:

  • You are replacing a gas water heater at all, unless your local code allows homeowner installation and you can have it inspected.
  • The vent type is changing, or the existing flue is corroded, undersized or shared with another appliance.
  • The new heater has a higher BTU input than the old one.
  • You smell gas. Leave the building first and call the gas utility from outside.

Never remove, plug or cap a T&P relief valve or its discharge pipe, and never operate a heater with the draft hood or vent disconnected.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important spec when comparing gas water heaters?

Vent type, because it decides what can be installed without major work. After that, compare first-hour rating, BTU input and UEF.

Is a power vent water heater better than an atmospheric one?

Not by default. A power vent solves the problem of a missing or unusable chimney. It needs electricity, will not run in a power outage, and its blower makes some noise. Where a sound vertical flue exists, an atmospheric or powered damper model is the simpler replacement.

Does a higher BTU water heater use more gas?

It burns gas faster while firing but fires for less time to heat the same water. Yearly gas use depends mainly on how much hot water you use and on the heater's UEF.

Can a natural gas water heater run on propane?

Only with the manufacturer's conversion kit for that model, installed by a qualified technician. If no kit is listed, buy the propane model.
